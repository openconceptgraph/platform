# Drafting prompt: completing cards across grades

**Version:** `draft-concepts/v2`. Every link and mistake drafted under this prompt cites it, so that a change of method is a new version, never an edit to this one. It builds on [`draft-concepts/v1`](draft-concepts-v1.md), whose tests for a concept, a family, a prerequisite and a typical mistake still apply. What follows is what v2 changes.

You are completing the interpretation of a slice of a base, a few weeks of teaching, so that every concept a lesson in that slice teaches or assumes has a complete card. The slice may span grades: the grade being taught, and the grade before it whose concepts its lessons assume. Everything you draft is still a proposal, reviewed one item at a time.

## One concept, every grade that teaches it

Concepts belong to the framework, not to a grade. Where two grades teach the same concept, the statements of both define it: a column method listed in Grade 7 and again in Grade 8 is one concept with two grades of defining statements, not two concepts.

- **Revise still never creates** (v1, test 5). A statement marked revise points back to the concept of the grade it revises. Where that grade is in the base, the concept is already defined there; where it is not, the gap is named in a rationale.
- **Clarification notes may define.** Where the content statement is a revise bullet but the curriculum's own notes set out what learners must know ("Learners should know and be able to use the following properties: ..."), those notes are the authority's words and may define the concept.

## Levels

Where siblings differ only by a parameter, such as the number range, the sign or the exponent, one concept has **ordered levels** (v1, test 2). Give each level a short **title** that reads after the concept's title ("with a positive and a negative integer"), and a description where the title is not enough. Levels go from easier to harder, in the order a teacher would take them. Do not give levels to a concept whose parts differ in their rule or their typical mistake: those are separate concepts.

## Sources

Every concept is defined by statements, word for word. Beyond that:

- **A prerequisite the curriculum states** cites the statement that states it, as **evidence**: "Learners should only explore the distributive property once they can multiply with integers." The link then cites the curriculum rather than the drafting run. A prerequisite the curriculum does not state cites the drafting run, with a rationale a teacher can check.
- **A typical mistake** cites, in this order of preference:
  1. the clarification note or exemplar where the curriculum itself names or warns against it (**evidence**);
  2. a published study that documents it (**publication**: the full citation, and a link where there is one). Cite a publication only where you can check that it exists and that it documents this mistake; never cite from memory alone;
  3. nothing: the mistake is drafted unsourced and flagged, for a reviewer who has seen it in class to source.

## Completeness

A card is complete when it has: its defining statements from every grade in the slice that teaches it, its kind and family, its levels where test 2 calls for them, one to three typical mistakes, and the prerequisite links a lesson on it depends on, within the slice and back into the grade before. Never draft a loop.
